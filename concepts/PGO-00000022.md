# Definition *(skos:definition)*

A contraindication is a disposition that increases the risk of harm involved in using a particular drug, carrying out a medical procedure, or engaging in a particular activity such that the risk of harm exceeds a threshold. An contraindication serves as a reason to withhold a certain medical treatment.

# Editorial notes

## Summary Expert Alignment *(dcterms:description)*

**Round 1** (2025-11-14)

The session reviewed multiple candidate definitions for Contraindication, including sources from OAE, MeSH, NCIT, and ISO IDMP. The group examined the conceptual relationship between Contraindication and Adverse Event — explicitly distinguishing the two: an adverse event is what may occur, while a contraindication is the reason not to proceed with a treatment.

The OAE definition (OAE_0000055) emerged as the clear preference: Darko Hric noted it offers "more descriptive language than others while keeping it precise"; Birgit Meldal agreed. The group acknowledged a mild wording issue — the phrase "An contraindication" contains a grammatical error (should be "A contraindication") and the definition has a degree of circularity — but these were deemed non-blocking.

The MeSH definition (absolute/relative split) was considered but judged as less precise. The NCIT C50646 definition was noted as qualitatively good but would require a label change. The ISO IDMP definition was rejected for being too drug-centric.

Post-session, the Steering Group confirmed the label as the single word "contraindication" (rejecting the hyphenated form "contra-indication") and requested an optional improvement: consider submitting an updated definition to OAE to reduce circularity. This optional action was flagged 2026-05-28; no status change was triggered.

**Outcome:** Consensus reached on OAE OAE_0000055 as the preferred definition. Label `Contraindication` confirmed.
