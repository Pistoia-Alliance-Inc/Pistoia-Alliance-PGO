# Editorial notes

## Summary Expert Alignment *(dcterms:description)*

**Round 1** (2024-08-01)

The first session reviewed multiple definitions of "protein" from various ontological and organisational sources to inform the PGO conceptual alignment.

The NCI Thesaurus ID C17021 was initially preferred by three of the funding partners as providing the most broadly applicable general definition. However, the discussion identified several limitations and divergences:

1. **Synthesis route** — a distinction between naturally occurring and synthetically produced proteins. NCIT_C17021 appears to refer to naturally occurring proteins only.
2. **Functional role** — ambiguity over whether proteins should be classified as therapeutic agents (e.g. monoclonal antibodies).
3. **Ontological role** — whether a protein is conceptualised as a substance or as a target entity in biomedical contexts.

The IDMP Ontology (IDMP-O) "protein substance" definition was noted but set aside: in IDMP-O, "substance" is a specification, which limits its utility for PGO's broader semantic scope. Adjacent domains (e.g. production) were flagged as relevant.

Organisational definitions were also considered:
- AstraZeneca provided a classical biochemical definition without a URI ("A Protein is a nitrogenous organic compound made of large molecules..."), emphasising structural and enzymatic functions.
- Roche offered a definition adapted from NCIt, focusing on genetically encoded entities with specific structural and functional properties.

The session did not reach final consensus and a follow-up was scheduled.

**Outcome:** Further input needed — follow-up session scheduled for 2024-08-15.

---

**Round 2** (2024-08-15)

The follow-up session addressed specific issues identified with NCIT_C17021:
- **Plural semantics**: the definition refers to proteins as a group rather than defining a single instance.
- **L- vs D-amino acids**: the definition explicitly mentions "alpha-L-amino acids", which excludes synthetic D-amino acid-containing peptides.
- **Natural protein constraint**: the definition is limited to naturally occurring proteins.

The Protein Ontology definition (PR:000000001 — "An amino acid chain that is produced de novo by ribosome-mediated translation of a genetically-encoded mRNA, and any derivatives thereof.") was discussed as an alternative but also found problematic for its focus on the natural synthetic route.

The expert group converged on a preference for definitions that describe the intrinsic, substance-based identity of proteins — rather than definitions constrained by method of synthesis or occurrence. AstraZeneca proposed the Molecular Interactions controlled vocabulary definition: *"A linear polymer of amino acids joined by peptide bonds in a specific sequence."* (MI_0326 — http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/MI_0326). This definition was noted for its generality, broad expert support, and compatibility with IDMP-O.

**Outcome:** Consensus reached on MI_0326 as the preferred definition. Formal recommendation submitted to the Steering Group.
