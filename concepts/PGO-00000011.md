# Editorial notes

## Summary Expert Alignment *(dcterms:description)*

**Round 1** (2024-08-29)

The session reviewed three main candidate definitions for "gene". The goal was to identify a broadly accepted and biologically accurate definition aligned with R&D and early development needs.

Three definitions were centrally discussed:

1. **NCIT C16612** — *"A functional unit of heredity which occupies a specific position on a particular chromosome and serves as the template for a product that contributes to a phenotype or a biological function."* Proposed by two PGO partners. Identified as the least controversial and most widely accepted formulation.

2. **Alternative NCIT formulation** — *"A functional unit of heredity that occupies a specific position (locus) on a particular chromosome, is capable of reproducing itself exactly at each cell division, and directs the formation of a protein or other product."* This version emphasises heritability and expression mechanisms; considered more precise but slightly narrower.

3. **Wikipedia / Schema.org** — *"A discrete unit of inheritance which affects one or more biological traits."* Noted as overly general and lacking specificity regarding molecular substrates (DNA/RNA).

The expert group acknowledged that additional clarification on the molecular basis (DNA, RNA) may be beneficial in future but agreed that NCIT C16612 effectively balances biological precision with general applicability across research and clinical domains.

**Outcome:** Consensus reached on NCIT C16612 as the preferred definition. Formal recommendation submitted to the Steering Group.
