# Definition *(skos:definition)*

Any substance which when absorbed into a living organism may modify one or more of its functions. The term is generally accepted for a substance taken for a therapeutic purpose, but is also commonly used for abused substances.

# Editorial notes

## Summary Expert Alignment *(dcterms:description)*

**Round 1** (2025-02-27)

The session explored the complex and context-dependent nature of "drug" as a concept in pharma R&ED.

Key discussion points:
- The DRON_00000005 (drug_product) definition was deemed unsatisfactory — it describes a product, not the drug itself.
- The group favoured BioLink_Drug and CHEBI_23888, both offering practical balance between generality and specificity.
- "Drug" may have enhancing properties, not just curative.
- The term "drug" changes meaning across pipeline stages (R&D, development, post-market).
- PGO core concepts "compound" and "product" are closely related; maintaining all three as distinct concepts was endorsed.
- Three options discussed: (a) adopt CHEBI_23888; (b) use "pharmacologic substance" as the core concept label with "drug" as synonym; (c) propose an amended definition to the ChEBI team.

**Outcome:** Preliminary preference for CHEBI_23888. Follow-up confirmation session scheduled.

---

**Round 2** (2025-05-29)

The session confirmed the Round 1 recommendation. Key conclusions:
- Keep "drug" as the concept label (along with "substance", "compound", "product") — as imprecise as they are, these terms are widely used in R&ED.
- CHEBI_23888 confirmed as the recommended definition.
- No change to the proposed recommendation from Round 1.

**Outcome:** Consensus confirmed on CHEBI_23888. Recommendation submitted to Steering Group.
