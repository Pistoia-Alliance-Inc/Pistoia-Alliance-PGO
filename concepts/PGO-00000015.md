# Editorial notes

## Summary Expert Alignment *(dcterms:description)*

**Round 1** (2024-08-29)

The session reviewed candidate definitions and immediately surfaced three key issues:
1. "Capable of breeding and producing fertile offspring" — unnecessarily restrictive; excludes asexual reproducers.
2. "Living" — potentially excludes viruses, which are not universally classified as living organisms.
3. **Virus inclusion is a must** — given the importance of viruses in pharma (vaccines, viral vector therapies).

The group requested a community email consultation to gather input on which reference definition could include "virus" in "species". Requests sent to all PGO experts on 2024-08-30.

**Outcome:** Email consultation initiated. Follow-up session scheduled.

---

**Round 2** (2024-09-11)

The session focused on selecting the most pragmatic definition accommodating viruses.

Key findings:
- The virus/species issue has not been resolved across the field.
- NCBI taxonomy (the global authority) defines species loosely and excludes virus by definition, although including virus within its subclasses. The group considered adopting NCBI Taxon vocabulary while noting this problem.
- The original NCBITaxon_species definition link was not found — NCBI does not formally define "species".
- UniProt Taxon definition (*"An element of a taxonomy for classifying life forms."*) was selected as:
  - Short and practical.
  - Does not include the breeding criterion.
  - Does not explicitly exclude virus (interpretation of "life forms" can accommodate viruses pragmatically).
  - CC BY 4.0 license.
- The group recommended that "virus" be explicitly acknowledged as considered part of the definition, even if the philosophical question about virus as a "life form" remains open.

**Outcome:** Consensus on UniProt Taxon definition. Virus inclusion acknowledged pragmatically. Recommendation submitted to Steering Group.
