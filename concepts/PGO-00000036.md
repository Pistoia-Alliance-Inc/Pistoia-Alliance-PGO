# Definition *(skos:definition)*

Any component of a drug product intended to exert pharmacological activity or other direct effect in the diagnosis, cure, mitigation, treatment, or prevention of disease, or to affect the structure or any function of the body of humans or other animals. (After 21 CFR 210.3(b)(7))

# Editorial notes

## Summary Expert Alignment *(dcterms:description)*

**Round 1** (2026-01-15)

API was the second concept discussed; shortlist identified but no consensus reached. Key debate: scope of "active" (active at the biological target vs. active in the body — relevance for prodrugs). New URI-less policy confirmed: PGO will mint its own W3ID identifiers. Shortlist at session close: SIO SIO_010338, AFO (row 6), FDA glossary (row 7, no URI), CAL (row 10). Birgit to consult Pfizer colleagues for additional SME input.

**Outcome:** No consensus. Shortlist produced. Deferred to Round 2.

---

**Round 2** (2026-01-29)

Continuation from Round 1. Key outcomes:

**Label:** Consensus reached — label should be `Active_Pharmaceutical_Ingredient` (API), not simply "active ingredient." All participants who expressed a preference agreed on this.

**Definition:** Debate narrowed to two candidates: (1) EMA/ICH Q7 definition — scientifically preferred, covers prodrugs (an expert confirmed importance of prodrug coverage), but lacks a resolvable URI; (2) NCIT C82533 / CDISC formulation — resolvable but shorter and displays alongside multiple definitions on the NCIT page.

Birgit noted: *"If it's not resolvable as an identifier, I just don't think we can use it."* Giovanni clarified that PGO has already agreed it can mint W3ID identifiers for URI-less definitions — this is may be the first concrete use case.

Christine Memmott: "substance" in the definition body is preferable to "ingredient" for broad modality coverage (proteins, antibodies, etc. are also APIs). Chris Day raised the ingredient/substance semantic distinction as a future modelling question, not a terminology blocker.

**Drug Substance introduced:** Strong expert lean toward treating Drug_Substance as a synonym of API (not a standalone concept). No formal decision reached — deferred to next session.

**Outcome:** Two options for SG — EMA/ICH Q7 definition (preferred text, no URI; PGO to mint W3ID) or NCIT C82533 / CDISC (resolvable fallback). Label = Active_Pharmaceutical_Ingredient. Drug Substance synonym question deferred.

---

**Round 3** (2026-02-12)

Primary focus: resolving the Drug_Substance / API synonym question.

An expert presented regulatory evidence: ICH Q7 (EMA/FDA harmonised GMP guideline, globally recognised) explicitly reads *"Active Pharmaceutical Ingredient or Drug Substance"* as an equivalence (page 44). An expert: *"Even I think it's the same. They can be used interchangeably in different contexts."*

Christine (nuanced view): acknowledged the regulatory equivalence but raised two edge cases — (1) pro-drugs in some internal SME interpretations; (2) non-small-molecule modalities (cell therapies, gene editing) where individual components may not be called APIs in the traditional sense. Concluded: *"I would probably collapse to one term."*

Darko: *"Drug substance can be considered slightly broader than API… but practically distinguishing the two we will probably not succeed."* Recommended noting as a known limitation and treating as equivalent.

**Confirmed recommendation for SG:**
- API definition: ICH Q7 / EMEA definition (no URI; accepted under new policy).
- Drug Substance = synonym of Active_Pharmaceutical_Ingredient. another concept (Drug_Substance) retained in register for traceability; SG to confirm formal disposition.

**Outcome:** Expert recommendation finalised. Ready for SG vote.
