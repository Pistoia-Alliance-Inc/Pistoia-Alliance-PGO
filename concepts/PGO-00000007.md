# Editorial notes

## Summary Expert Alignment *(dcterms:description)*

**Round 1** (2025-03-06)

The session jointly evaluated "Compound" and "Substance" to clarify their conceptual relationship. Key findings:
- "Compound" appears to have a more precise, accurate chemical composition than "Substance"; "Chemical compound" was noted as a possibly more accurate label but compatible with NCIT_C43366.
- The semantic boundaries between "compound", "substance", "drug", and "product" were acknowledged as context-dependent and partially overlapping.
- Initial convergence on NCIT_C43366 as the least controversial definition.

**Outcome:** Initial consensus on NCIT_C43366. Follow-up session scheduled.

---

**Round 2** (2025-03-25)

The round 2 session confirmed NCIT_C43366 as the least controversial candidate. The group acknowledged the definition is not ideal — "substance", "element", and "ingredient" are imprecise terms — but no better candidate with a resolvable identifier and permissive licence was identified. The label "compound" was confirmed as appropriate (over "chemical compound"), aligned with common pharma R&D usage.

The group also discussed examples to illustrate the distinction between compound, drug, and product:
- acetylsalicylic acid (as compound) vs. aspirin (as product)
- acetaminophen (as compound) vs. paracetamol (as product)
- semaglutide (as compound/drug)

**Outcome:** Consensus confirmed on NCIT_C43366. Recommendation submitted to Steering Group.
